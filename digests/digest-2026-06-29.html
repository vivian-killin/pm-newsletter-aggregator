Ornith-1.0, released by DeepReinforce, is a self-scaffolding LLM built on Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.5 with multiple size variants (9B Dense, 31B Dense, 35B MoE, 397B MoE). It achieves state-of-the-art performance on coding benchmarks among open-source models of comparable size, making it competitive with larger proprietary models for agentic coding tasks.

For product teams, this matters because open models reduce vendor lock-in risk, lower inference costs at scale, and enable fine-tuning for domain-specific agent behavior. If your PM thesis includes long-term AI agent features, you now have a credible path to self-hosted inference that's not dependent on OpenAI or Anthropic's API availability and pricing.

Read more

Gergely catalogs how engineering culture has evolved in the last six months: tooling maturity, organizational structure changes, and a surprising trend toward deliberate deceleration. Companies like Stripe and others are investing more in design, discovery, and alignment before shipping—a counterintuitive move in an AI era where feature velocity is technically unlimited.

The insight is that when your bottleneck shifts from implementation to insight, "going slow" is actually the fastest path. Teams that take time to understand user problems deeply, test hypotheses rigorously, and align stakeholders before shipping end up shipping better products faster than teams that ship constantly and iterate on feedback. In an AI-native product world, this becomes the competitive edge.

Worth your time regardless of when it was published
Why it's worth your timeThe "Build to Learn vs Build to Earn" framework is essential reading for PM strategy in the AI age. Cagan argues that discovery (build to learn) is fundamentally different from delivery (build to earn), and as AI makes delivery cheaper, your competitive advantage shifts entirely to discovery. This is the conceptual foundation for everything happening in the Gusto case study above.
Read more

Marty distinguishes two modes: the project model (roadmap → features → delivery) and the discovery model (problem → hypothesis → experiment → insight). In a traditional world with high implementation cost, teams conflated them. But AI has made delivery so cheap that the constraint is no longer "can we build it?" but "are we building the right thing?"

PMs who master discovery in the AI era—who can run rapid experiments, synthesize user insight, and validate hypotheses before implementation—will own product strategy. This is why Gusto's "no specs, tight feedback loops" model works: the team isn't discovering through slow specification cycles; they're discovering through rapid prototyping with an AI partner that executes at prototype speed.
