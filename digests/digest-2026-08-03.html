Why a leading CPO believes the role itself may be problematic
Why it's worth your timeTom Verrilli's contrarian take challenges conventional PM org structures. For AI-native teams moving fast, his argument for fewer, more senior PMs doing hands-on work over scaled teams is directly relevant to how you should staff product in 2026.
Read more

Verrilli argues that traditional PM org scaling—hiring more junior PMs to hit staffing ratios—often produces worse outcomes than having a smaller team of senior, IC-level product thinkers. This is especially acute in AI products where the pace of experimentation and the technical depth required favor deep ownership over delegation.

The insight applies squarely to AI-native product: when your team is shipping agents, fine-tuning models, and iterating on prompts weekly, you need PMs who can think in systems, code when necessary, and make trade-offs independently. Ratio-based thinking is a relic.

The trap teams fall into when shipping faster doesn't mean shipping better
Why it's worth your timeThe AI Productivity Paradox cuts to the core tension every PM faces in 2026: teams are clearly shipping more with AI, yet outcomes aren't improving. Understanding why exposes where your PM thinking needs to shift.
Read more

The paradox is real and well-documented by McKinsey and others: companies leveraging AI tools see velocity spike but business outcomes plateau or decline. Why? Because shipping faster without better strategic thinking, customer discovery, or prioritization just amplifies the wrong bets. An AI-generated feature shipped twice as fast is still a wrong feature.

For product leaders in the AI era, this is a critical reframe: your job isn't to enable teams to ship more. It's to ensure they ship the right things, and AI tools should unlock clearer thinking, faster validation, and better customer insight—not just faster coding. If you're not measuring this distinction, you're caught in the productivity trap.
