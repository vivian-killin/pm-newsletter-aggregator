In light of all that has changed, what are the things that you used to argue were true, that you no longer believe?
TL;DR — PM TakeawayStrong convictions without humility become liabilities in fast-shifting markets. PMs should actively audit which core beliefs still hold in an AI era, rather than defending past positions.

Cagan reflects on a keynote question that forced him to examine which product principles he once championed but no longer fully endorses. The piece is structured as a narrative exploration of how conviction and openness to change must coexist.

Rather than listing specific beliefs, Cagan frames the exercise as a discipline: regularly questioning your own orthodoxy, especially when the operating environment has fundamentally shifted. This is directly relevant to PMs grappling with AI's impact on prioritization, discovery, and team structure.

Pedro Franceschi

September 13
How to think of building agents as onboarding AI employees with a specific job, skill set, manager, and budget.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayStop treating AI agents as tools and start treating them as team members with defined roles and responsibilities. This reframe changes how you spec, measure, and iterate on agentic products.
Read more

Franceschi (CEO of Brex) presents a mental model for building and deploying AI agents: define their job, equip them with specific skills, assign them to a manager (human or system), and set their operational budget. This moves beyond the chatbot paradigm into genuine delegation and autonomy.

The episode includes a live demo showing how this framework applies to real workflows. For PMs, this is crucial context for designing AI products that integrate into teams rather than sitting as isolated assistants.

Vinoo Ganesh

September 12
Before co-founding Kepler, Vinoo Ganesh led Spark at Palantir and built Project Frontline—a pioneering program for Forward Deployed Engineers.
TL;PR TakeawayForward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) embed deep product expertise in customer environments and act as a feedback loop to engineering. This organizational model becomes more critical as products grow in complexity and require tight customer integration.
Read more

Ganesh walks through the FDE model: engineers who are stationed at or deeply integrated with customers, solving real problems in real time and bringing learnings back to the product org. He covers hiring criteria, skill development, and the best practices for running an effective FDE program at scale.

For PMs managing complex or AI-driven products, FDEs represent a way to collapse the distance between product decisions and customer outcome data. This is particularly valuable in agentic or autonomous systems where failure modes are harder to predict in lab settings.

Itamar Gilad

August 12
Worth your time regardless of when it was published
Why it's worth your timeDefines the gap between "doing something competently" and "being able to judge if it's right"—the core challenge for PMs in an AI era where execution speed is no longer the bottleneck.
Read more

Gilad contrasts artificial competence (AI can now do most tasks passably) with genuine judgment (knowing whether the task should be done at all, and whether it's done well). As a former Google PM, he frames this through the lens of organizational skill gaps: you're not good at everything, but you can often hire or delegate. AI changes this equation—it's cheap and fast but unreliable in judgment calls.

For product teams, this means the scarcest resource shifts from execution to discernment. The PM role becomes even more about taste, prioritization, and knowing what *not* to build.
