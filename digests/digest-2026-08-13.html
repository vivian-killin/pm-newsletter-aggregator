Lenny Rachitsky

Three founder archetypes and how to turn your origin story into a communications strategy
TL;DR — PM TakeawayYour origin story isn't just narrative—it's a product positioning tool. Lenny maps three founder archetypes and their natural communication strengths. Identify which archetype you are, lean into it authentically, and use it to make your product mission memorable and believable to customers and investors.
Read more

Lenny identifies three founder archetypes: the Visionary (sees the future first), the Problem-Solver (obsessed with fixing a specific pain), and the Craftsperson (driven by doing work better). Each has a natural story shape and audience resonance. The Visionary works best with investors and believers; the Problem-Solver converts skeptics through lived credibility; the Craftsperson builds loyalty through standards and taste.

For PMs, this means your product narrative should match your team's authentic archetype. Forcing a Visionary story when you're a Problem-Solver kills credibility. The takeaway: audit your current communication. Are you leaning into your natural strength, or fighting it? Align your roadmap narrative, customer messaging, and internal strategy around the archetype that's actually driving your product.

Gergely's conversation with Charity explores why 2025's healthy caution has flipped. The evidence is in: AI coding assistants ship faster, reduce rote work, and actually improve code quality when used well. The question for PMs is no longer "should we use AI?" but "how do we embed it into discovery, planning, and execution without creating over-reliance or losing critical thinking?"

The edge case: teams that blindly accept AI output see productivity gains evaporate. Teams that treat AI as a junior collaborator—requiring review, context, and architectural ownership—see sustained improvements. For product leaders, this means investing in how your team thinks about AI rather than just adopting the latest model.
