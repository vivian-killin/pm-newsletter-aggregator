Article 1 — "You'll Lose Your Job in 2027" — Elena Verna via Lenny's Newsletter

Elena Verna's cross-post is the bluntest, most actionable career survival guide circulating right now. The premise: even if you keep your seat, your actual job — responsibilities, tools, scope, team size — is being rewritten every day. Her advice isn't to panic. It's to prepare now, before someone else decides what version of your job survives. "I technically stayed at the same company. But I do not have the same job."

Her action plan: first, be honest about whether you like what you do (competition gets more intense as AI levels the field, and you won't beat someone who loves the work). Second, build career optionality. Third, and most actionable: become AI-native. She lays this out as a concrete 6-tier ladder:

🔢 Elena Verna's 6 Tiers of AI-Nativeness

  1. Tier 1 — Writing Buddy: Use AI to improve written communication. Write your own version first, then have AI critique it. Prompts: "Make this more clear" / "Rewrite this so a skeptical exec would buy in."
  2. Tier 2 — Meeting Partner: AI in every meeting — writes todos, tracks commitments. Prompts: "If you were my manager, what are my strengths and areas for improvement?"
  3. Tier 3 — Thinking Partner: LLMs will "Great point!" you to death if you let them. Explicitly demand disagreement. Prompts: "Disagree with me. What's the strongest counter-argument?" / "What am I missing?"
  4. Tier 4 — Builder: Internal tools, prototypes, landing pages. Prompts: "Build me a working prototype of this flow."
  5. Tier 5 — Shipping to Prod: Non-technical people pushing code to production. Instant job security upgrade.
  6. Tier 6 — Agents: Automations running on a schedule with real oversight. Warning: "That AI chatbot on your site is NOT an agent."
"AI Strategy decks? Straight. To. Jail. If you don't know what matters, what good looks like, or where to focus… AI just helps you do the wrong thing faster."

Article 2 — "The AI Paradox: More Automation, More Humans, More Work" — Dan Shipper on Lenny's Podcast

Dan Shipper (CEO of Every, 30 people, 7-figure ARR) returned to Lenny's podcast with contrarian takes grounded in running an actual AI-native company. Every single person at Every uses AI heavily — and yet their headcount doubled this year and SaaS spend is up.

  1. The future of work will happen inside Codex or Claude Code — not inside your SaaS tools.
  2. Every company will have one "super-agent" in Slack — not one per employee.
  3. SaaS is not dead — he's bullish on SaaS stocks. Agents need SaaS seats, which adds demand.
  4. PMs will thrive — "Any PM who gets really AI-native will be incredibly dangerous, because the building is done for you — what matters is figuring out what to build and if it's great."
  5. Full-stack designers are becoming superheroes — they can now ship their vision without the handoff bottleneck.
  6. No AI job apocalypse — but you must "ride the models." "Models make yesterday's human competence cheap."
  7. Automation is a lie — every automation needs a human to manage it.
  8. Build for humans and agents to use together — new design requirements: approval flows, inboxes that summarize, logs, easy rollback.
  9. We will read more AI-generated writing and like it — for internal docs and strategy.
  10. Forward deployed engineer is the new must-have role — hands-on, loves maintaining internal agents.

John Cutler

May 23
AI Won't Save the Kingdoms We Built
TL;DR — PM Takeaway Before pitching "AI-powered cross-functional alignment" to your exec team, read this. The bottleneck in most orgs isn't information friction — it's that no one wants to own the hard tradeoffs. AI surfaces context faster, but it doesn't make the decision. Ask "who decides?" before asking "how do we share more information?"
Read more

Cutler published his sharpest essay in months. The setup: the same leaders who built bloated ZIRP-era org structures are now promising that AI will tear them down. He doesn't buy it.

"Anyone working in those environments felt the problem viscerally. Dependencies compounded. The end-to-end experience became harder to reason about. One team's work created a blast radius for another team. The customer did not experience the org chart as cleanly as the org chart experienced itself."

His key argument on AI as savior: "Context transfer is not the same thing as shared understanding. AI does not clarify who decides. AI does not neutralize incentive misalignment. AI does not resolve contested ownership."

His warning about "coordination theater": people with a bias toward working alone now have a new quiver of avoidance tactics — set up agents, send copious docs, let agents talk to each other. The front lines are now drowning in async noise instead of org-chart politics. Same problem, new shape.

The real opportunity: maybe, finally, we make the hard org choices that should have been made anyway. "AI can amplify a coherent operating model. It cannot substitute for one."

🔥 Deep Pull: Elena Verna's 6-Tier AI-Nativeness Framework

The single most actionable thing from this week's research is Elena Verna's 6-tier framework, published May 21. It's the clearest mental model circulating right now for where you actually are on AI adoption — not where you claim to be.

Most PMs are at Tier 1-2. The Tier 3 jump — using AI as a genuine thinking partner that challenges your logic — is where the real leverage shift happens. You have to explicitly demand disagreement: "Disagree with me. What's the strongest counter-argument?" is the prompt that transforms a yes-machine into a thinking partner.

The Tier 5-6 leap (shipping to prod, running agents) is where PMs become genuinely irreplaceable — because the people who can define what the agent should do, verify its outputs, and manage its failure modes are exactly the people with product sense and domain context.
