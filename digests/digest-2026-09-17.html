Delivery isn't free—all things product podcast with Teresa Torres & Petra Wille
TL;DR — PM TakeawayThe industry refrain "AI makes delivery free" masks real costs: quality assurance, evals, iteration cycles, and human judgment all still require investment. Don't let the speed of AI generation trick you into underestimating the work required to ship a quality product.
Read more

Torres and Petra Wille deconstruct one of the most repeated claims in product management right now—that AI has eliminated the cost of delivery. They argue this oversimplifies how AI-powered products actually work in practice.

The core insight: fast generation is not the same as fast shipping. AI can produce candidate solutions, copy, code, or designs in seconds, but validating, editing, testing, and refining those outputs still demands human time and attention. Teams that treat AI output as final are setting themselves up for quality issues and rework.

TL;DR — PM TakeawayAI-generated opportunity trees can overwhelm PMs with noise. Focus your evals on the highest-confidence, actionable opportunities rather than surfacing every possible variant—otherwise your team spends cycles sorting signal from AI-generated static.
Read more

A Vistaly customer using AI-generated opportunity solution trees ran into a practical problem: the tool surfaced 4 new evals and 16 experiment variants from a single customer complaint. The volume of options was paralyzing, not helpful.

The lesson for PMs: AI can generate alternatives at scale, but that doesn't mean all of them deserve equal weight in your roadmap decision-making. Build evals that filter for quality and strategic fit, not just comprehensiveness. Your team's capacity to act is still finite.

Muse review: The personal AI agent that gets consumer UX right
TL;DR — PM TakeawayVo tests Meta's Muse, a personal AI agent that handles calendar, email, and family coordination—and ships beautiful, usable output in a single pass. For PMs building AI agents, this is a masterclass in constraint-driven design: focus on a narrow, high-value job and do it with taste and polish rather than breadth.
Read more

Vo gave Muse access to her calendar, email, and her children's schedules, and it produced a single-shot family PDF of higher quality than anything she'd made manually with Claude or Codex. The review highlights how Muse succeeds where many AI agents fail: it assumes the user has limited time and attention, and it delivers a finished artifact rather than a conversation.

The product lesson: personal AI agents don't need to be fully general-purpose. They win by picking a specific outcome (a beautifully formatted family calendar), accepting the user's context as input, and shipping something the user can use immediately without editing.

Peter Yang

Sep 16
My 3-step system to automate 90% of my content workflow with AI
TL;DR — PM TakeawayYang converted a 15-step manual content process into 8 reusable AI skills, saving 5+ hours per week. The framework—decompose, skill-ify, orchestrate—is directly applicable to how PMs should architect AI workflows for their teams.
Read more

Rather than replacing humans wholesale, Yang broke his workflow into discrete skills (research, outline, draft, edit, etc.) and assigned each to an AI tool or prompt-based agent. The result is a system that's faster and more predictable than doing everything manually, but also more controllable and auditable than a single black-box solution.

The PM lesson: the highest leverage automation isn't about finding a tool that does everything. It's about mapping your workflow, identifying which steps are repeatable and rules-based, and building a skill stack that lets humans focus on judgment calls while AI handles the mechanical work.
