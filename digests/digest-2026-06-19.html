Lenny Rachitsky

June 17
Design AI agent loops: schedules, goals, and subagents with live builds that run autonomously.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayStop treating agents as one-shot tools. Lenny breaks down four loop types—heartbeats, crons, goals, and subagents—that turn agents into self-managing systems. Use this framework to design loops that earn ownership of tasks without human re-triggering, and encode your product's decision logic into runnable agent flows.
Read more

The video covers every loop type with clear explanations and two live Claude Code and Codex builds that demonstrate autonomous PR management in action. Heartbeat loops run on fixed intervals; cron loops are scheduler-driven; goal loops self-trigger when milestones are hit; subagent loops delegate work to smaller, specialized agents.

The insight for PMs: once you move beyond prompt-and-wait workflows, you shift from "agent as assistant" to "agent as stakeholder." Your roadmap needs to model not just what agents should do, but the cadence and conditions under which they act. This changes how you scope work, define success, and integrate agents into CI/CD and product workflows.

Simon Willison

June 18
Datasette Apps: sandboxed HTML+JavaScript applications hosted inside Datasette for building custom interfaces.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayDatasette Apps let you build interactive, custom UIs without leaving your data layer—powerful for data-heavy products where UI tooling has historically been the bottleneck. If your product roadmap includes reporting, dashboards, or multi-user data tools, this plugin model shows how to let customers build on top of your platform without creating a new deployment surface.
Read more

Datasette Apps are self-contained HTML+JavaScript applications that run inside a tightly constrained iframe sandbox. They can query the Datasette API, manipulate data, and present custom interfaces—all without needing a separate backend or new infrastructure. The sandbox keeps them safe and isolated.

This architecture has PM implications: it's a case study in turning a data platform into a platform-for-platforms. Combined with the ACL 0.6a0 release (also this week), which expands resource-sharing to general permissions, you're looking at a pattern for building multi-tenant, extensible data products that don't require you to own every UI layer your users need.

Open models finally become a real frontier story as GLM-5.2 passes the vibe check against proprietary alternatives.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayIf you've been betting your roadmap on closed-model APIs, GLM-5.2's release (753B parameters, open weights, MIT license) signals a shift: the cost and autonomy math for open models is now viable. For PMs building AI-native products, this means revisiting your model strategy—custom fine-tuning, on-prem deployment, and vendor independence are no longer distant dreams.
Read more

Z.ai released GLM-5.2 on June 16 with 40 active parameters and MIT-licensed open weights. Swyx's framing: "the open models story finally becomes a real frontier story." The benchmark and vibe-check data suggest it's competitive with or exceeding GPT-class performance on many tasks.

For PMs: this doesn't mean you need to switch immediately, but it means your lock-in assumptions are weakening. If your product's differentiation depends on proprietary model access, you have a window to build moats elsewhere—on the product workflow, on data pipelines, on domain-specific fine-tuning, or on integration depth. The era of treating LLM capability as a scarce, vendor-locked resource is ending.

For PMs, the takeaway is: add a regulatory review gate to your feature roadmap, especially if you're targeting healthcare, finance, or government sectors. The cost of a feature ban is now priced into your shipping calculus.

Why it's worth your timeEvals are the modern PRD: Ankur Goyal from Braintrust explains how to encode taste and product standards into systems that scale. If you're shipping AI products, this is how you operationalize "quality" without growing QA linearly.
Read more

Braintrust uses AI agents, evals, and CI to ship software with fewer humans in the loop. The insight: instead of writing specs for humans to follow, you write evals that your agents optimize against. This flips the PM workflow—your roadmap becomes a set of measurable properties that agents must preserve, rather than a list of features engineers must build.

For AI-native PMs, this is the template for scaling without drowning in complexity. Your job shifts from "tell people what to build" to "define what good looks like, measure it, let agents iterate."
