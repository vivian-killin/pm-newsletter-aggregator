From hand-drawn sketch to 3D-printed gown: how Codex plus computer use makes previously unmanufacturable garments possible for a solo founder.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayYana Welinder's case demonstrates how agentic AI tools (code interpreters + LLMs) can collapse the technical barrier to full-stack product building. For PMs, this signals that the definition of "minimum viable team" has shifted—single non-technical founders can now own the entire loop from design to manufacturing integration. Consider how your product onboarding and feature complexity assumptions may need to change for an AI-augmented user base.
Read more

Yana Welinder, a solo fashion founder, built both custom 3D garment design software and a full e-commerce business using primarily Codex (for code generation) and ChatGPT's computer use capabilities. She moved from hand-drawn sketches to parametric 3D models that could be 3D-printed, all without hiring a single engineer. This represents a qualitative shift: she didn't just automate tasks, she restructured her entire workflow around what AI can do natively.

The implications for PM thinking are profound. First, feature prioritization changes when your users have AI agents at their side—you may need to expose more underlying logic rather than hiding complexity. Second, the notion of "technical founder" is being redefined. Third, this creates new market categories: products that were previously only viable for large teams (bespoke manufacturing + digital storefronts) are now solo-founder accessible.

Astro creator Fred Schott discusses why agents are defined by their harnesses, and why he added React-style hooks to the Flue framework.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayFred Schott's Flue 2 brings stateful composition patterns (React hooks) to agentic systems, suggesting that agent architecture is maturing toward component-like abstractions. For PMs building agent-native products, this implies that the UX and orchestration of multi-step workflows will soon shift from prompt engineering toward compositional frameworks. Invest in thinking about how your product can be broken into agent-harness abstractions rather than monolithic flows.
Read more

Flue 2 is a framework for building AI agents that borrows architectural patterns from React: hooks for state management, composable harnesses for agent behavior, and a focus on reusability. Schott argues that agents shouldn't be thought of as single entities but as compositions of specialized behavioral harnesses, each with its own state lifecycle.

This is a significant PM signal because it suggests the tooling layer is moving away from "prompt and pray" toward structured, composable agent design. If this pattern takes hold, product teams will need to think about how their workflows decompose into agent capabilities, not just task sequences.

For PMs, this reframes the productivity conversation: AI doesn't obsolete design judgment, it accelerates the cycle of hypothesis and feedback. The competitive advantage shifts to teams that can use AI to test more design ideas faster, not teams that can skip design thinking altogether.

Why it's worth your timeA Fresh Definition of the Product Role cuts through the noise of job descriptions and operating models to argue that the core PM competency is learning to work effectively in your specific context. As AI reshapes team structures and skill requirements, this grounding principle—context-adaptive product thinking—remains more relevant than ever.
Read more

Cagan's thesis is that the "product role" has no fixed shape because different companies, stages, and market conditions demand different things. What matters is that a PM understands their organization's constraints and can navigate them effectively. In the age of AI, this becomes even more critical: the PM who can articulate where humans drive leverage versus where AI handles execution will become the most valuable.

This is a timely antidote to the AI-era panic that "everyone is becoming a product manager." They may be—but that doesn't change the fundamental PM skill: understanding trade-offs and context deeply enough to make decisions that stick.
