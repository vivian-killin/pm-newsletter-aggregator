Rich Mironov

August 20
Technical Ingredients, Revenue Ingredients
Why it's worth your timeMironov cuts through the myth that faster development velocity equals faster revenue by distinguishing executable code from commercially deployable software from actual revenue-ready products—a critical forcing function for teams tempted to ship AI-accelerated outputs prematurely.
Read more

Mironov addresses a common failure mode: solo builders and executives confusing shipping code with building products. He introduces a three-tier checklist that separates technical delivery from commercial readiness. The insight is deceptively simple but rarely applied: you can have fast code velocity and still ship nothing that generates revenue if GTM, compliance, support, and pricing work aren't staffed and progressing in parallel.

For PMs in AI-heavy environments where engineering output has accelerated, this framework becomes essential push-back ammunition against leadership assumptions that throughput alone drives business results.

Teresa Torres

July 14
Quality of Evidence — All Things Product Podcast with Teresa Torres & Petra Wille
Why it's worth your timeTorres introduces a "ladder of evidence" framework for distinguishing weak signals (support tickets, sales notes) from actionable customer insight (story-based interviews), directly applicable to audit your discovery practice regardless of AI adoption.
Read more

The core tension: teams drowning in behavioral analytics, support tickets, and feedback inboxes often skip direct user conversations, assuming the data speaks for itself. Torres and Wille argue that this conflates quantity with quality. A support ticket showing what broke is not the same as a story interview capturing what the user was trying to do, why it failed, and what they needed instead.

The ladder of evidence provides a concrete way to audit what your team currently treats as "customer research" and push toward the higher-fidelity methods that drive better product decisions—especially important as AI tools make it easier to generate outputs that feel like research but aren't.
