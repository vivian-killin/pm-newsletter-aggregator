Lenny Rachitsky

Nov 15
How Meta shipped the fastest-growing consumer AI product in history
TL;DR — PM TakeawayMona Sarantakos reveals that Muse's success came from two core levers: identifying the right activation moment and using "agent swarms" to systematically surface user insights at scale—a model for how PMs should think about research and onboarding in AI-native products.
Read more

Muse started as a weekend experiment with OpenClaw on a Raspberry Pi, but Meta waited seven months before launch—a disciplined approach that contrasts with the AI race mentality. Sarantakos, VP of Product at Meta Superintelligence Labs, walked through how the team discovered and iterated on the product, including an unexpected use case that became core to adoption.

Key insights: (1) Agent swarms—running multiple AI agents in parallel—allowed the team to reach every user and surface patterns no traditional survey could find. (2) The team identified two specific levers that move activation for an AI agent: the entry point and the first interaction quality. (3) Even placeholder elements (Jolly, the avatar) became iconic, suggesting that in early AI products, personality and approachability matter as much as capability. (4) Sarantakos looks for PMs who can hold tension between speed and rigor, especially when shipping agentic experiences where misalignment can compound.

Build an AI customer research agent that emails every customer who drops off, with evals to keep it safe in production
TL;PR — PM TakeawayGreg Greiner (ex-CPO at Bolt, now CEO of Woven) went from talking to 7-8 customers per month to reaching every single one—using an AI agent that runs research at scale. The pattern is replicable: identify the moment of drop-off, let Claude email & synthesize responses, and add evals to prevent drift.
Read more

Aakash walks through a real case: Ancestry.com saw customers buying DNA kits but dropping after. Rather than run a traditional survey, they had an AI agent reach out via email and phone. Result: 3,470 replies that surfaced friction surveys never would have caught. The agent synthesized patterns in real time, letting the product team iterate faster on the actual problem, not the assumed one.

The tutorial covers: (1) why AI research outperforms traditional in-app surveys (it catches the moments when users are most engaged with your product), (2) the six moments in a user's funnel to reach out, (3) step-by-step Claude Code setup, and (4) how to layer evals—automated checks that keep the agent honest in production. For PMs, this is a scalability pattern: use AI to do the listening work that used to consume weeks, then apply your judgment to the insights.

Why it's worth your timeGilad diagnoses five structural reasons product discovery fails—even in companies that claim to value it—and argues that AI development hype is making the problem worse by enabling faster shipping without the evidence to back it up.
Read more

Gilad observes that many organizations recognize discovery is better than waterfall, yet few execute it rigorously. The failures are systematic: unclear success metrics, shallow user research, misaligned incentives, weak experimentation discipline, and executives overruling data with intuition. AI acceleration is exacerbating this—teams can now prototype and ship faster than ever, but the discovery muscle hasn't kept pace. The result is more features, less traction.

For PMs in 2026, this is especially relevant: as AI tools make iteration faster, the discipline of *actually knowing what moves the needle* becomes your competitive advantage, not your bottleneck.
