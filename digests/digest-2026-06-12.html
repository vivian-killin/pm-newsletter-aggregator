Early access review of Claude Fable 5: capabilities and critical gaps
TL;DR — PM TakeawayEvaluate frontier models on real use cases, not benchmarks. Fable 5 excels at some tasks but has notable limitations—understanding where new models genuinely shine (and where they fail) prevents building features on fragile foundations.
Read more

Lenny gained early access to Claude Fable 5 and stress-tested it against Anthropic's marketing claims. The core finding: Fable is significantly more capable in certain domains but has glaring gaps in others. What Anthropic gets right includes improved reasoning, multimodal handling, and autonomy. What goes very wrong includes reliability issues on specific task types and a tendency to hallucinate under pressure.

For PMs, the lesson is methodological: don't rely on press releases or benchmark scores. Run targeted experiments on your actual product use cases before committing roadmap capacity to an AI feature. The best PMs treating Fable as a real tool (not a category shift) and stress-testing it in production scenarios before launch.

Willison's key observation: Fable is "relentlessly proactive." It will spot problems, generate solutions, and execute them autonomously. He illustrates this with a live example fixing a UI glitch in Datasette—Fable identified and corrected the issue without being asked. This is powerful but also raises UX questions.

For AI-native PMs, this creates a design tension: increased autonomy is appealing, but it can also reduce user agency. The best products will need explicit guardrails around when and how models take proactive action. Willison's deeper point is that this trait changes how you think about error recovery, undo, and explainability in AI features.

Three emerging patterns in AI infrastructure and model design
TL;DR — PM TakeawayThree concepts shifting the landscape: Loopcraft (stacking loop patterns for agent design), the Model Labs vs Agent Labs split (different team structures for different problems), and untrainability (what you can't solve through training). Understanding these frameworks helps PMs think structurally about AI roadmaps rather than feature-by-feature.
Read more

Swyx's coverage highlights key architectural thinking from Karpathy and others. Loopcraft emphasizes composable loop patterns (perception-action-reflection cycles) as a design primitive. The Model Labs vs Agent Labs distinction matters: some problems are solved through better models; others need smarter agentic workflows. Untrainability surfaces constraints that no amount of fine-tuning will solve.

For PMs, this is strategic. Before committing to training or fine-tuning a model, ask: is this a model problem or an agent problem? The answer changes your roadmap, hiring, and partnership decisions. These frameworks also help avoid the trap of throwing compute at problems that need architectural fixes.

Gergely Orosz

Jun 11
Anthropic's Fable restrictions are shifting competitive dynamics, plus trends in model routing
TL;DR — PM TakeawayFable's usage restrictions are creating an unexpected opening for competitors—users will route around limitations if alternatives exist. This signals a broader trend: model choice is becoming a product feature, not a one-time decision. Build flexibility into your AI architecture now.
Read more

Orosz covers the market reaction to Fable's controversial usage policies. The core insight: overly restrictive models are being abandoned by users, not accepted. This is creating demand for smarter model routing (choosing the right model for each task) and multi-model stacks. He also flags emerging patterns in enterprise reliability (Coinbase's failover gaps, for example) that will matter as AI becomes core infrastructure.

For PMs, the implication is clear: single-model dependencies are a liability. Design for model flexibility and smart routing from day one. The cost of switching models mid-product is high; the cost of being locked into a model with unacceptable constraints is higher.
