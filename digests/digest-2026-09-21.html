90 minutes of unfiltered product advice from Snap and Discord's product chief | Peter Sellis
TL;DR — PM TakeawayPeter Sellis breaks down why median PMs fail: they optimize for defensibility instead of impact, misunderstand how growth actually happens (hint: it's rarely from new features), and lack the psychological resilience to push great people hard. His framework of three "oxymorons" of great product management offers a concrete model for thinking about your own leadership gaps.
Read more

Sellis draws from seven years building Snapchat's core product and leading product at Discord during explosive growth phases. He discusses why he designs teams like "terrorist organizations"—small, autonomous, mission-driven units—and why managing exceptional talent (he cites his experience with Nikita Bier) requires pushing people harder than they expect, not lighter.

The conversation covers why Snapchat's ads business never reached its potential despite early advantages, what separates the top 5% of PMs from everyone else, and the counterintuitive insight that growth almost always comes from making the core product irresistible, not from adding new features. Sellis emphasizes the importance of being unfiltered and willing to make unpopular calls based on evidence rather than consensus.

Ethan Mollick

Sep 18
The Overhang
TL;DR — PM TakeawayMollick argues that knowledge workers—especially PMs—are sitting on untapped potential because they haven't yet learned to combine deep expertise with AI agents in ways that create disproportionate impact. The "overhang" is the gap between what you *could* ship and what you're currently shipping; closing it requires taste, agency, and willingness to fail.
Read more

Mollick explores how the arrival of capable AI agents has created an unusual moment: people with real domain knowledge can now leverage AI to operate at a scale and speed that wasn't possible before. But most aren't doing it yet—either because they don't believe they can, or because they're waiting for permission or perfect tools.

For PMs, the insight is personal: your understanding of your product, your users, and your market is an asset that AI can amplify dramatically if you actually use it to make decisions and ship experiments faster. The overhang isn't a product problem; it's a mindset problem about what you're capable of now.

Jev represents a new direction in AI product strategy: moving away from the assumption that bigger, more general models are always better. Instead, TypeSafe AI is building models tuned for specific production tasks—better latency, lower cost, higher accuracy on domain-specific problems.

For PMs building AI products, this is a critical signal that the era of "just use GPT-4" is ending.
