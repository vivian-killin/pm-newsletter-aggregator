Why every company now needs to think and operate like a lab team
TL;DR — PM TakeawayJosh Woodward (Google Labs) explains that rapid idea generation, ruthless killing, and knowing when to quit are now core PM skills. Teams need structured ways to run small experiments fast and recognize genuine product-market fit early—not just launch and iterate blindly.
Read more

Woodward describes Google Labs' "almost possible" framework for finding ideas that are just beyond reach today but likely within reach in 6-12 months. The key insight for PMs: the pace of change in AI means your competitive window is measured in weeks, not quarters. You need a culture that can explore many directions simultaneously, validate rigorously, and abandon ideas without ego.

He also emphasizes that roles are not blurring into one—specialists still matter—but specialists need to operate inside labs teams with fast feedback loops. PMs lead this by setting up decision gates early (not waiting for final polish) and building psychological safety around killing ideas.

Two practitioner guides on agentic workflows and AI evaluations
TL;DR — PM TakeawayPMs shipping AI agents need to understand how to build evals and workflows that prove agents actually work. Hamel Husain's crash course on evaluations and Micky Shimeles' 4-step engineering workflow show concrete ways to move from hype to reliable agent products.
Read more

Micky Shimeles walks through a 4-step agentic engineering workflow focused on shipping real applications. Hamel Husain digs into how to surface genuine AI failures through evals—using Claude Code and Codex to automate the detection work rather than manual testing. Both frame the problem as: how do you know your agents are ready for production?

For PMs: this is the operational side of agent-powered products. You need to shift from "does it work?" (yes/no) to "when does it fail?" and build that visibility into your roadmap gates. These are the skill gaps blocking most teams from shipping agent products at scale.

Swyx

Oct 12, Oct 10
How Anthropic's Dynamic Workflows and Standard Bots show the next shape of AI products
TL;DR — PM TakeawayAnthropic's design of multi-agent workflows (inspired by Unix/Linux philosophy) and Standard Bots' approach to reliable AI execution offer blueprints for building AI products that scale. PMs should study how these teams decompose complex tasks into simpler, provable pieces.
Read more

Richard MacManus covers Anthropic's dynamic workflows, which coordinate Claude agents by separating concerns—each agent handles one task well, and the workflow manager orchestrates them. This mirrors Unix's "do one thing well" principle. It's a PM-relevant insight because it shifts the design conversation from "how powerful is the model?" to "how do we compose modular, testable agent pieces?"

Standard Bots demonstrates the same principle in physical robotics: pretrained models learn from human demonstrations, then improve through corrections from real deployments. For product teams, the lesson is structural: build feedback loops that let your AI system learn from failure in production, not just in training.
