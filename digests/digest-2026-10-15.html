Building an AI Voice Agent for 360 Degree Feedback: How Your360 AI Uses State Machines, Evals, and Layered Synthesis
TL;DR — PM TakeawaySustaining long agent conversations requires architecture beyond simple prompts: state machines keep context coherent across 44-minute calls, layered synthesis handles conflicting signals from multiple speakers, and rigorous evals catch failures before users do. Design your agent's decision tree and evaluation strategy before you write a single prompt.
Read more

Your360 AI built a voice agent that conducts 360-degree feedback interviews—holding space for candid, emotionally complex conversations that last nearly an hour. That's not a chatbot; it's a structured system.

Torres unpacks how they used state machines to manage conversation flow without losing thread, evals to validate that the agent actually understood what it heard, and layered synthesis to reconcile contradictory feedback from multiple raters into coherent insights. The conversation design itself is the product—not the AI model.

Julie Zhuo

October 13
What would make AI truly social?
TL;DR — PM TakeawaySocial software thrives when it solves the friction of bringing people together—but AI agents risk recreating the isolation we already have. Design agents as facilitators of human connection, not replacements for it, if you want adoption that actually compounds over time.
Read more

Zhuo opens with a striking trend: dinner party attendance in America has collapsed from 7.1 per year in the mid-1970s to nearly half that by the late 1990s. Social software promised to fix this but largely accelerated isolation instead. She argues that consumer AI agents face the same trap—they can make interaction frictionless, but frictionless doesn't mean meaningful.

The blueprint she sketches is agents that solve real coordination problems between humans: organizing, facilitating, mediating disagreement. Not agents that absorb the interaction entirely. The PM angle is clear: if your agent removes the need for humans to show up for each other, you've built something that feels helpful but leaves users lonelier.

Peter Yang

October 15
Is Software Dead, or Just Not for Humans Anymore?
TL;DR — PM TakeawayTraditional software's defensibility is eroding as AI commoditizes features and UX. The real value is migrating toward infrastructure, automation, and workflows that serve agents—not just humans. PMs building for 2026+ need to think about what humans and agents need to do together, not in sequence.
Read more

Yang argues that the software business model we've relied on—defensible features, sticky UX, proprietary data—is collapsing under AI pressure. Any feature you can describe, Claude or GPT can replicate in minutes. The money is flowing instead to infrastructure that agents run on, end-to-end workflows that coordinate human and machine work, and systems that get smarter as agents interact with them.

The product implication: if you're building software only for humans, you're fighting a race to the bottom. If you're building for the human-agent collaboration layer—where a person decides what matters and an agent executes it—you have room to differentiate.

Swyx

October 14
Crossing the Threshold: How OpenAI and Anthropic Approach Autonomy and Safety in Financial Agents
TL;DR — PM TakeawayFrontier labs are converging on a design pattern: give agents real autonomy for low-risk decisions, build approval gates for consequential ones, and use this tiering to ship fast without creating liability. For PMs shipping agents into regulated domains, this threshold model is the practical framework that wins adoption.
Read more

At AI Engineer New York, both OpenAI and Anthropic showed how they're thinking about giving financial agents the autonomy to move money or execute trades without human intervention—while keeping humans in the loop for decisions that matter. The pattern isn't "always ask," it's "autonomy by risk tier."

Low-impact decisions (categorize this transaction, suggest a rebalance) run fully automated. Medium-impact (transfer funds between accounts) get a review step. High-impact (liquidate investments) require explicit approval. Swyx reports this reduces friction for users while maintaining the safety guarantee that regulators and enterprise buyers actually care about. The PM takeaway: tier your agent's actions by consequence, not by capability.
