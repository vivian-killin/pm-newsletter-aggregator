Why Netflix is betting on systems thinkers in the AI era, and how Alex Lieberman built a Claude workflow that preserves voice at scale
TL;DR — PM TakeawayHire and develop systems thinkers who see across functions rather than deep specialists. For AI content workflows, the secret isn't the model—it's the *system* around it: interview loops to capture voice, structured Markdown prompts, and multi-persona revision loops that prevent output from sounding generic.

Elizabeth Stone shares Netflix's operating philosophy: in the AI era, the ability to connect dots across product, engineering, data, and strategy matters more than being the best at any one discipline. Systems thinkers ship faster and make better bets on which AI capabilities drive real leverage.

Separately, Alex Lieberman (Morning Brew founder) walks through his exact Claude workflow: he records voice memos that get transcribed, then Claude interviews him before drafting, codes his personality traits in Markdown, and runs a six-persona revision loop (editor, copywriter, brand voice, etc.) to keep content authentic. The result: 4+ newsletters weekly that read human, not synthetic.

Gergely Orosz

Jul 15-16
Context engineering: how to structure AI assistance so code quality doesn't suffer, plus a privacy disaster in Grok's CLI
TL;DR — PM TakeawayContext engineering is the underrated practice of shaping the inputs to coding agents so they produce higher-quality output without hallucination or slop. For PMs shipping AI-assisted developer tools, this is your lever: help teams structure their code context, docs, and prompts—not just rely on the model.

Dex Horthy explains context engineering: it's the discipline of curating exactly the right code snippets, architecture docs, and prompt structure so AI agents have high-fidelity information. Without it, agents make plausible but wrong suggestions. With it, they integrate seamlessly into existing codebases. This reframes the AI coding-assistant market: your differentiation is workflow design, not model power.

Separately, Gergely reports that Grok's CLI was caught uploading local files to the cloud without user consent—a reminder that for security-conscious enterprises, build transparency and audit trails into your AI tooling from day one.

Why it's worth your timeBen Thompson's case for legalizing fair-use model training and enabling US open models to compete. For PMs in AI infrastructure, this maps the geopolitical and legal winds that will shape your market in the next 18 months.

Thompson proposes: (1) make data collection for model training explicitly fair use, and (2) bar restrictions on model distillation. The logic is that labs trained on unlicensed internet data while forbidding others from doing the same—hypocrisy that weakens US open-source competitiveness against China. This is worth understanding because regulatory clarity on training data and distillation will reshape the economics of every AI product company.
