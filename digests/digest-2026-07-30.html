Gergely Orosz

A deepdive on what's changed in how the leading AI lab makes software.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayAI code review and testing is now a production reality at scale at Anthropic—not a gimmick. Larger implications: PMs need to rethink quality gates, reviewer-to-engineer ratios, and what "testing coverage" means in an AI-augmented workflow.
Read more

Orosz interviews Anthropic engineers and reveals that AI-assisted code review and test writing have become core to their development process. The key insight: two-pizza team structures remain intact, but the team's ability to move through review cycles and catch edge cases has fundamentally changed. Engineers report that Cursor and similar tools accelerate the drafting phase, while AI reviewers handle boilerplate and pattern matching, freeing humans for architectural and business logic review.

For PMs, the lesson is structural: if AI can reliably handle routine code review, your bottleneck isn't review anymore—it's requirement clarity and design decisions upstream. This shifts how you staff teams, how you think about velocity metrics, and what kinds of bugs make it to production.

Simon Willison

On writing as a skill-building exercise, not just an output.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayBruce Schneier's insight—that writing assignments are "gym tasks" for critical thinking—reframes how you should approach documentation, spec writing, and even PRD drafts in the age of AI. The act of writing itself builds judgment; outsourcing it entirely may undermine the decision-making muscle you need as a PM.
Read more

Willison quotes Schneier on why writing policy memos isn't about the memos themselves—it's about the thinking that happens in the outlining, drafting, and editing phases. In a world where AI can generate specs and docs in seconds, this is a critical challenge for PMs: if you delegate all writing to AI, you may gain speed but lose the thinking process that actually builds your strategic instincts.

The implication for product leaders is subtle but important: encourage your team to write PRDs and strategy docs themselves, even if AI could do it faster. The friction is the feature.

Swyx

Jul 30
AI engineers are rediscovering ontologies as a way to keep probabilistic agents inside deterministic boundaries.
TL;DR — PM TakeawayOntologies (structured knowledge representations) are becoming foundational infrastructure for production agentic systems. As a PM building agent-based products, understanding ontology-driven design helps you scope agent authority, prevent hallucination in critical paths, and scale reliability.
Read more

Swyx reports that after years of the semantic web being written off, AI engineers are actively rebuilding structured knowledge systems to constrain agent behavior. Instead of pure RAG or prompt-based agents, production teams are using ontologies to define what an agent can and can't do, what entities exist, and what relationships are valid. This is the inverse of the "just scale up the model" philosophy—it's structured reasoning making a comeback.

For PMs: this is critical for safety and reliability in agent products. A well-designed ontology becomes your product's "policy layer"—it's how you operationalize trust and control without constant human intervention.

Worth your time regardless of when it was published
Why it's worth your timeThe AI Productivity Paradox cuts through the hype: teams are shipping faster with AI tools, but business outcomes haven't improved. This is the most important meta-question PMs need to answer right now—are you optimizing for velocity or impact?
Read more

Cagan surfaces the McKinsey finding that many organizations report faster delivery and lower costs from AI, yet their actual business results (user adoption, revenue, retention) are flat or declining. The paradox suggests that teams are doing the wrong things faster—shipping features that don't matter, or solving problems that don't exist for users.

The implication for your roadmap: AI is a force multiplier on execution quality, not a substitute for discovery. Before you let AI accelerate your team, make sure you've actually validated that you're building the right thing. Otherwise you're just shipping wrong things at scale.
