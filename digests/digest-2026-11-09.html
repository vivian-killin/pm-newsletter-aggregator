Marty Cagan

Nov 06
How AI is redefining product management roles and responsibilities
TL;DR — PM TakeawayThe product manager role is evolving fundamentally under AI. Rather than waiting for tools to stabilize, PMs need to actively reshape their responsibilities around what AI changes—moving from execution oversight toward deeper discovery, judgment, and stakeholder orchestration as AI handles more tactical work.
Read more

Cagan's keynote addresses the core tension: the product role has always varied widely across companies, but AI is accelerating a fundamental redefinition. He argues that PMs who cling to traditional definitions of their role risk becoming irrelevant, while those who proactively rethink what product management means in an AI-native organization position themselves as indispensable.

The piece explores how responsibilities are shifting—less time spent on requirements documentation and roadmap presentations, more time on deep customer discovery, building judgment around which problems matter, and learning to partner effectively with AI engineering teams. Cagan frames this as opportunity, not threat: PMs who embrace this shift become strategic leaders within their organizations.

Lenny Rachitsky

Nov 08
Tulsee Doshi on what product management actually looks like inside frontier AI labs
TL;DR — PM TakeawayTulsee Doshi, Head of Product for Gemini, explains that frontier AI PM work is less about feature prioritization and more about evals, iterative model improvement, and shipping ahead of the curve. Her 60-20-20 framework—60% on what works now, 20% on what's almost working, 20% on experiments—reveals how product thinking operates at the model layer.
Read more

Doshi discusses how product management inside a frontier AI lab inverts traditional assumptions. Instead of waiting for requirements, PMs are embedded in research and engineering, making judgment calls on which capabilities matter most. She describes model development as more art than engineering—"like Snape's potions class"—where intuition, evals, and user feedback combine to guide direction.

The conversation covers Google's full-stack frontier AI strategy and the rising value of experienced PMs in these settings. Doshi emphasizes that great PMs at this level combine deep technical intuition, an ability to run rigorous evals, and the judgment to balance shipping working features against investing in speculative capabilities—a skill set very different from traditional product roles.

This deep dive reveals the emerging market structure around frontier model development. Handshake's success shows that PMs building for AI labs need to understand not just what data these organizations need, but how they integrate data into model training pipelines and evaluation workflows. The piece includes specific examples across finance, bio, and alignment work.

The insight for product builders: the AI infrastructure market is rapidly segmenting. Generalist data providers are losing to specialists who deeply understand the workflows and pain points of their buyer—frontier labs building specific model capabilities. This mirrors broader patterns where AI-native products succeed by obsessing over a narrow set of use cases rather than chasing broad applicability.

The piece meditates on how the internet has changed—from personal, quirky, human-scaled sites to algorithmic feeds and optimized experiences. Zhuo explores what happens when everything is designed for maximum engagement and efficiency. It's a meditation on taste, intentionality, and what gets lost when products optimize purely for metrics.

For PMs in 2026, this is a useful counterweight to the relentless pressure to AI-ify every interaction. Sometimes constraints, slowness, and human idiosyncrasy create experiences that feel more valuable precisely because they haven't been algorithmed into blandness. Worth revisiting when your roadmap is full of agent-based automation and personalization.
