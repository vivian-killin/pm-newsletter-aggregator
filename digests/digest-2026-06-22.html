How Claude Mythos found a 15-year-old bug in Mozilla Firefox and what it teaches us about AI agent design
TL;DR — PM Takeaway

The real leverage in AI agents isn't the model alone—it's the goal-loop harness and orchestration layer. Brian Grinstead's work at Mozilla shows that workflow design, feedback loops, and task decomposition are what turn raw model capability into production results. As a PM, invest in understanding and shaping the agent loop, not just the LLM.

Read more

Brian Grinstead from Mozilla discusses how Claude Mythos, paired with the right harness architecture, was able to identify and help fix 423 security vulnerabilities in a single month—including a critical 15-year-old bug that human engineers had missed.

The key insight: the model was only half the battle. The goal-loop harness—how the agent breaks down the problem, iterates, receives feedback, and focuses its attention—was equally critical to the outcome. This has direct implications for how PMs should think about building agent-powered products: the quality of your agentic workflow often matters more than the quality of your base model.

The process demonstrates how Claude Code handles multi-step technical problems iteratively, debugging and refining until the model works in a web environment.

The meta-lesson: shipping AI features is becoming less about deep specialized knowledge and more about effective agent-human collaboration. For PMs shipping AI products, this means your teams can now iterate faster on AI-native features without hiring specialist ML engineers for every port or optimization.

AI security is not cybersecurity with a coat of paint. Zico Kolter and Gray Swan's approach shows that effective red-teaming and safety for agentic systems requires new thinking—adversarial robustness, interpretability constraints, and behavioral guardrails that traditional infosec didn't need to consider. For PMs shipping agents to users, this is table-stakes.

Read more

Zico Kolter, an OpenAI boardmember and AI safety expert, joins Swyx to discuss why the security model for agentic AI systems fundamentally differs from traditional cybersecurity. Gray Swan's approach to red-teaming focuses on understanding how agents might fail, misbehave, or be manipulated—not just how external attackers might breach infrastructure.

The conversation highlights that as agents become more autonomous and goal-directed, the safety surface expands. PMs need to bake in red-teaming and adversarial testing into the product roadmap early, not as an afterthought.

Build to Learn vs Build to Earn — reframing product development in the age of AI
Why it's worth your time

Marty's framework separates discovery (build to learn) from delivery (build to earn). In an AI-native world where the cost of building has plummeted, this distinction becomes critical for PM thinking. Most teams are still in "build to earn" mode; the winners will be those disciplined about discovery.

Read more

Marty argues that the traditional project/roadmap model conflates building to discover what works with building to deliver what you've already validated. As AI tools lower the cost of building, the real bottleneck shifts: you can build anything, so the question becomes, how do you know what to build?
