Anthropic's first technical PM on token maxing, the jagged edge, and living in the future
TL;DR — PM TakeawayDianne Penn reveals how Anthropic's eval-driven development loop—measuring specific capabilities in a repeatable way—became the PM playbook for shipping competitive AI. The key insight: make narrow bets measurable, then let engineering compound them into defensible products.
Read more

Penn was Anthropic's first PM and led the decision-making that shaped Claude's competitive positioning. She discusses the "jagged edge" concept—the uneven performance profile of LLMs—and how PMs must think differently about product roadmaps when your product has both superhuman and subhuman capabilities in different domains.

The core PM thesis: rather than chase every benchmark, identify high-leverage use cases (like coding), build evals that measure progress in those specific areas, and use the eval results to drive engineering prioritization. This creates a virtuous cycle where product focus drives research focus.

An inside look at the relay market powering token resellers and fraud
TL;DR — PM TakeawayA hidden arbitrage market has emerged where resellers discount LLM API access by pooling keys and routing requests through proxies. For PMs: this reveals customer willingness to pay and emerging security/compliance risks if your pricing doesn't reflect real customer economics.
Read more

Matt Lenhard's investigation uncovers a largely China-based market where token resellers offer 30–50% discounts on API pricing by abusing free trial accounts, shared keys, and proxy routing. This isn't just cost arbitrage—it's a signal that some customers see official API pricing as uncompetitive.

The PM implication: if a meaningful cohort is willing to add latency and security risk to save on tokens, your pricing model may not be calibrated to customer value. Tiered pricing, volume discounts, or usage-based models might recapture this cohort and reduce fraud incentive.

The AI Productivity Paradox recognizes a pattern across the industry: product teams using AI are delivering more features, more quickly, yet customer satisfaction and business metrics remain flat or decline. Cagan argues this is because speed without strategy compounds bad decisions faster.

For your team: AI is a multiplier. If your discovery process is weak, AI amplifies that weakness into more shipped waste. Before measuring "features shipped per sprint," measure "discovery conversations per feature" and "customer feedback loops per release." Make discovery faster with AI, not just execution.
