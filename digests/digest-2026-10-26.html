An 86-minute guide to finding PMF | Rob Snyder (Author, The Power of Pull)
TL;DR — PM TakeawayRob Snyder's PULL framework—Project, Unavoidable, List of options, Limitations—cuts through the noise of finding product-market fit by grounding discovery in real customer constraints rather than designer preferences. PMs can apply this inside larger companies by reframing how they approach problem validation and sales conversations.
Read more

Rob Snyder, who has advised 50+ startups to $1M ARR, walks through a repeatable framework for moving from idea to validated product-market fit. The PULL method starts by identifying a specific project (what the customer is trying to accomplish), then determining what makes it unavoidable (why they must solve it now), building a list of alternative solutions they might consider, and finally understanding their constraints (budget, timeline, technical limits).

The framework flips traditional product thinking: instead of asking "What would customers want?" Snyder emphasizes cold outreach, running first sales calls, and entering what he calls "goblin mode"—scrappy, willing-to-fail iteration when nothing works. He also cautions against design partners early on, since they rarely represent the broader market. For PMs in larger organizations, the PULL framework translates to how product teams validate problems before committing engineering resources.
